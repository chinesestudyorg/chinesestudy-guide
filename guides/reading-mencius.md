# Reading Mencius: questions, analogies, and arguments

[Home](../README.md) · [Chinese philosophy](chinese-philosophy.md) · [Web reading guide](https://chinesestudyorg.github.io/guides/reading-mencius/)

A conversation can change direction without changing speakers. Follow the question Mencius receives, the example he introduces, and the conclusion he asks a ruler to accept.

## Choose one exchange, not a whole doctrine

The current ChineseStudy [Mencius contents](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/mengzi/) offer 23 sections from Liang Hui Wang I and II. Start with one exchange. The [Mencius introduction](https://chinesestudy.org/en/figures/mencius/) gives background, but keep the ruler’s immediate question beside your notes. The published selection is not the whole received Mencius.

## Notice how the question changes

In [Why Speak of Gain?](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/mengzi/01-he-bi-yue-li/), the king asks what will benefit his state. Mencius turns to benevolence and what is right, then considers what happens when different ranks pursue their own advantage. Write two separate sentences: what the king asks, and what Mencius wants him to consider. This keeps the reply from shrinking into an isolated slogan.

In the original, locate 利, 仁, and 義 or 义. Use the English translation to follow the exchange, then return to each occurrence. Ask whose interest is under discussion and whether the same English term serves every sentence equally well.

## Try a four-line argument map

Write the ruler’s question, Mencius’s example or reason, the conclusion, and one doubt of your own. Add the passage URL and reading language. If an arrow between two steps is unclear, mark it as a question instead of supplying a missing premise without saying so.
