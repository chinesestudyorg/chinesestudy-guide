# How to Read Tang Poetry with Original Text and Translation

[Home](../README.md) · [Getting started](getting-started.md)

The [Three Hundred Tang Poems reading edition](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/tangshi-sanbai/) is a place to explore poems alongside their Chinese text and reading support. For an initial reading, focus on what happens inside one poem: the scene it presents, the speaker’s position, and what changes between the beginning and the end.

## Read a scene before interpreting it

Start with [Li Bai’s Drinking Alone under the Moon](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/tangshi-sanbai/004-yue-xia-du-zhuo/). List the concrete things mentioned in the poem. Then ask which are present in the scene and which become companions through the speaker’s imagination.

Read the translation, return to the Chinese lines, and mark where the speaker’s relationship to those companions changes. This gives you a question to bring to the commentary instead of treating the poem as a single abstract statement about loneliness.

## Compare two poets

Explore the reading paths for [Li Bai](https://chinesestudy.org/en/figures/li-bai/) and [Wang Wei](https://chinesestudy.org/en/figures/wang-wei/), then choose one poem from each. Compare a specific detail: how a landscape is introduced, how movement appears, or whether another person enters the scene.

The comparison is a reading exercise. Let the passages support what you say; two poems cannot establish everything about either poet’s work.

## Use sound carefully

Read the lines aloud with pinyin if it helps you follow the characters. Listen for repetition and pauses. The pronunciation support is a practical aid for present-day readers; it does not by itself explain historical rhyme or reconstruct Tang-era pronunciation.

## Keep a small reading record

- Poem title and page URL.
- One image or expression you want to revisit.
- A brief summary of how the scene or feeling changes.
- One question to check against the notes or another translation.

Continue through the [Chinese Tang poetry edition](https://chinesestudy.org/zh-hans/books/tangshi-sanbai/) if you prefer modern Chinese explanations. For help comparing the text with a translation, see [Using pinyin and translation](pinyin-and-translation.md).
