# A Week of Reading Chinese Classics

[Home](../README.md) · [Getting started](getting-started.md)

Use this suggested plan if you want a small reading task for each day. It combines two texts with deliberate rereading. Keep one note for each session; familiarity with a passage is more useful than finishing a long list of pages.

Allow about fifteen minutes per session, or divide a session across two days. The schedule is a reading exercise, not a promise that classical Chinese will become easy in a week.

## Seven reading sessions

| Session | Text or activity | Your task |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | [Li Bai’s Drinking Alone under the Moon](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/tangshi-sanbai/004-yue-xia-du-zhuo/) | Read the original and translation. Identify the speaker and list the things in the scene. |
| 2 | Return to the same poem | Use pinyin to follow the lines aloud. Choose one expression, locate it in the translation, and consult the notes. |
| 3 | Reread and summarize | Describe how the speaker’s relation to his imagined companions changes. Keep the passage beside your explanation. |
| 4 | [Tao Te Ching chapter 1](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/dao-de-jing/01/) | Read the translation, then find repeated terms in the original. Focus on 道 or 名. |
| 5 | Return to the same chapter | Compare two occurrences of your chosen term. Record the wording of the translation and a question it leaves open. |
| 6 | Revisit the commentary | Read the notes that address your question. Write what they explain and what you still want to examine. |
| 7 | Review your reading notes | Reread both texts. Choose one question for next week, then browse [the Analects](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/lunyu/) or the [Wang Wei reading path](https://chinesestudy.org/en/figures/wang-wei/). |

If you prefer modern Chinese explanations, use [《月下独酌》](https://chinesestudy.org/zh-hans/books/tangshi-sanbai/004-yue-xia-du-zhuo/) and [《道德经》第 1 章](https://chinesestudy.org/zh-hans/books/dao-de-jing/01/). Keep the reading task the same and change the language of your notes.

## A reusable reading note

Copy this structure into your notebook. Quote only the short phrase needed for your question, and keep the source link with it.

```text
Work and passage:
Source URL and reading language:
Phrase I studied:
What the translation explains:
What the notes add:
My summary in one or two sentences:
Question for my next reading:
```

The final question matters even when you have not settled an interpretation. A precise question gives you a reason to return to the text and something to compare with your next reading.

For reference tools, explore [Awesome Chinese Classics](https://github.com/chinesestudyorg/awesome-chinese-classics). Choose a dictionary or text collection according to the question you are trying to answer; a character database, a dictionary entry, and a translation serve different purposes.
