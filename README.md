## New reading paths

The live library now includes [Mencius](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/mengzi/), with 23 sections from Liang Hui Wang I and II, and the seven Inner Chapters of [Zhuangzi](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/zhuangzi/). These are the published selections, rather than claims of complete editions of either work. The Tang poetry catalogue lists 306 poems; use its contents to choose a form or an author, rather than reading it as a fixed beginner sequence.

- **Follow an argument:** begin with [Why Speak of Gain?](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/mengzi/01-he-bi-yue-li/) and ask how Mencius changes the king’s question. Continue with the [Mencius reading guide](guides/reading-mencius.md).
- **Read a story:** open [Nurturing Life](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/zhuangzi/03-yang-sheng-zhu/), find Cook Ding’s account, and distinguish what he does from what the ruler learns. Use the [Zhuangzi reading guide](guides/reading-zhuangzi.md) for a focused exercise.

## A practical way to begin

1. Choose one short poem or passage. Avoid starting with a whole book.
2. Look at the original Chinese and use pinyin to follow pronunciation.
3. Read the translation, then return to the original to see what it helps explain.
4. Consult the notes for unfamiliar expressions and context. Summarize the passage in your own words.

For a first session, try [Li Bai’s Drinking Alone under the Moon](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/tangshi-sanbai/004-yue-xia-du-zhuo/) or [chapter 1 of the Tao Te Ching](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/dao-de-jing/01/).

## Reading controls

Reading pages let you toggle pinyin and translation, adjust text size and line spacing, and choose dark mode. Use these controls to keep the original in view at a comfortable size. The page’s Feedback link lets you report a problem with its language and passage context.

## Languages

Read through the [English](https://chinesestudy.org/en/) or [简体中文](https://chinesestudy.org/zh-hans/) entrance. The [Spanish entrance](https://chinesestudy.org/es/) offers selected reading paths for Confucius, Laozi, and Sun Tzu; coverage varies by work and language.

Classical Chinese differs from modern Mandarin. Pinyin helps you pronounce characters, while translations and notes help you examine meaning. Neither replaces careful reading of the original.
