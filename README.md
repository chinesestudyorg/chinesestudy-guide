## A practical way to begin

1. Choose one short poem or passage. Avoid starting with a whole book.
2. Look at the original Chinese and use pinyin to follow pronunciation.
3. Read the translation, then return to the original to see what it helps explain.
4. Consult the notes for unfamiliar expressions and context. Summarize the passage in your own words.

For a first session, try [Li Bai’s Drinking Alone under the Moon](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/tangshi-sanbai/004-yue-xia-du-zhuo/) or [chapter 1 of the Tao Te Ching](https://chinesestudy.org/en/books/dao-de-jing/01/).

## Languages

Read through the [English](https://chinesestudy.org/en/) or [简体中文](https://chinesestudy.org/zh-hans/) entrance. The [Spanish entrance](https://chinesestudy.org/es/) offers selected reading paths for Confucius, Laozi, and Sun Tzu; coverage varies by work and language.

Classical Chinese differs from modern Mandarin. Pinyin helps you pronounce characters, while translations and notes help you examine meaning. Neither replaces careful reading of the original.
